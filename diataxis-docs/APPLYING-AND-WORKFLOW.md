# Applying Diátaxis and Workflow

How to put Diátaxis into practice and work with it effectively.

---

## Getting Started

Diátaxis is primarily intended as a pragmatic approach for people working on documentation. Most of the key principles required to put it into practice successfully can be grasped intuitively.

**Don't wait to understand Diátaxis before you start trying to put it into practice.** Not only do you not need to understand it all to make use of it, you will not understand it until you have started using it (this itself is a Diátaxis principle).

As soon as you feel you have picked up an idea that seems worth applying to your work, try applying it. Come back here when you need more clarity or reassurance. Iterate between your work and reflecting on your work.

## Principles of Working with Diátaxis

### 1. Use Diátaxis as a Guide, Not a Plan

Diátaxis describes a complete picture of documentation. However, the structure it proposes is not intended to be a **plan**, something you must complete in your documentation. It's a **guide**, a map to help you check that you're in the right place and going in the right direction.

The point of Diátaxis is to give you a way to think about and understand your documentation, so that you can make better sense of:
- What it's doing
- What you're trying to do with it
- Where its problems lie
- What you can do to improve it

It provides tools that help assess it, identify where its problems lie, and judge what you can do to improve it.

### 2. Work Iteratively

Don't try to complete everything at once. Work on one piece at a time, improve it, publish it, then move on to the next.

Diátaxis strongly prescribes a structure, but whatever the state of your existing documentation - even if it's a complete mess by any standards - it's always possible to improve it, **iteratively**.

### 3. Don't Work on the Big Picture

Focus on small, concrete improvements. The big picture will emerge from many small steps in the right direction.

Although Diátaxis is intended to provide a big picture of documentation, **don't try to work on the big picture**. It's both unnecessary and unhelpful. Diátaxis is designed to guide small steps; keep taking small steps to arrive where you want to go.

### 4. Publish Immediately

Every step in the right direction is worth publishing. Don't wait for perfection - publish improvements as you make them.

It's natural to want to complete large tranches of work before you publish them, so that you have something substantial to show each time. **Avoid this temptation** - every step in the right direction is worth publishing immediately.

## A Typical Workflow

### Step 1: Identify What You Have

Look at your existing documentation:
- What pieces do you have?
- What type is each piece? (Use [The Compass](./THE-COMPASS.md))
- Where does each piece fit on [The Map](./THE-MAP.md)?

### Step 2: Identify What's Missing

Check [The Map](./THE-MAP.md):
- Which quadrants are empty or sparse?
- Which types of documentation are missing?
- What are your users asking for?

### Step 3: Pick One Piece to Work On

Choose based on:
- **User needs**: What are people struggling with?
- **Impact**: What will help the most users?
- **Feasibility**: What can you improve quickly?
- **Your knowledge**: What do you understand well?

### Step 4: Determine the Type

Use [The Compass](./THE-COMPASS.md) to determine:
- What type should this be?
- Is it currently the right type?
- Does it need to be split into multiple types?

### Step 5: Improve the Piece

Follow the guidance for that type:
- [Tutorials](./TUTORIALS.md)
- [How-to Guides](./HOW-TO-GUIDES.md)
- [Reference](./REFERENCE.md)
- [Explanation](./EXPLANATION.md)

### Step 6: Publish

Don't wait for perfection:
- Publish the improvement
- Get feedback
- Iterate

### Step 7: Repeat

Go back to Step 3 and pick the next piece to work on.

## Working with Existing Documentation

### When Documentation is a Mess

If your documentation is disorganized:
1. **Don't try to fix it all at once**
2. **Pick one piece** that's causing problems
3. **Identify what type it should be** (use The Compass)
4. **Improve it** following the type's guidance
5. **Publish it**
6. **Repeat**

### When Documentation is Mixed

If a piece mixes multiple types:
1. **Identify the primary purpose** (use The Compass)
2. **Split it** if it's trying to do too much
3. **Focus each piece** on one type
4. **Link between pieces** if they're related

### When Documentation is Wrong Type

If a piece is the wrong type:
1. **Identify what type it should be** (use The Compass)
2. **Rewrite it** following that type's guidance
3. **Don't try to convert everything** - work piece by piece

## Starting from Scratch

If you're starting new documentation:
1. **Start with what users need most** (use The Compass to identify)
2. **Write one piece** of that type
3. **Publish it**
4. **Get feedback**
5. **Add more pieces** as needed

Don't try to create all four types at once. Start where the need is greatest.

## Working with a Team

### Divide by Type

Different people might be good at different types:
- Some people are good at writing Tutorials
- Some people are good at writing Reference
- Some people are good at writing How-to Guides
- Some people are good at writing Explanation

Let people work on what they're good at.

### Review by Type

When reviewing documentation:
- Check that it follows the type's guidance
- Check that it's the right type (use The Compass)
- Check that it's clear which type it is

### Coordinate Through The Map

Use [The Map](./THE-MAP.md) to:
- See what's being worked on
- Identify gaps
- Coordinate coverage

## Practical Steps

1. **Identify what you have** - Look at your existing documentation and try to categorize it into the four types

2. **Identify what's missing** - Which types are underrepresented or missing entirely?

3. **Pick one piece to improve** - Don't try to fix everything at once

4. **Use the compass** - When you're unsure which type something should be, use the compass to find direction

5. **Publish immediately** - Don't wait for perfection. Every improvement is worth publishing.

6. **Iterate** - Come back and improve more pieces over time

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Trying to complete everything at once** - Work iteratively
- **Treating Diátaxis as a checklist** - It's a guide, not a plan
- **Waiting to understand everything** - Start applying it immediately
- **Holding back improvements** - Publish every step in the right direction
- **Working on the big picture** - Focus on small, concrete improvements
- **Not using The Compass** - Guessing what type something should be

## Measuring Progress

Don't measure progress by:
- How many pieces you've written
- Whether you have all four types
- How complete your documentation is

Measure progress by:
- Are users able to accomplish their goals?
- Are users finding what they need?
- Are users understanding the system?
- Are users learning effectively?

## Remember

- **Work iteratively** - One piece at a time
- **Use Diátaxis as a guide** - Not a checklist
- **Focus on small improvements** - Not the big picture
- **Publish immediately** - Don't wait for perfection
- **Listen to users** - They'll tell you what's needed
- **The best way to learn Diátaxis is by doing** - Start applying it now, and come back to these guides as you need them
